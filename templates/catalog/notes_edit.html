How to use

Writing

Type in the editor. Press Enter for a new line. Use Export PDF to see the finished, paginated document.

Formatting

Use the toolbar for bold, italic, underline, text colour, highlight, font, size, alignment and links. Select your text first, then click a button.

Inserting math

Click any symbol or structure in the toolbar (fractions, integrals, Greek letters, matrices, and so on). A small editor opens as a drop-down anchored to the spot — fill in the boxes, then click away or press × to save. The math is added to the page as a chip. Drag the drop-down by the handle at its top to move it, or drag any edge to resize it; open several at once if you like.

Editing math

  • Click a chip to reopen and edit it.
  • Press Backspace next to a chip to delete the whole thing at once.

Typing math directly

Type LaTeX and it turns into a chip automatically once you close the delimiter:

  • $x^2$ or \( ... \) — inline math

To centre an equation, put it on its own line and click Align center (↔ C) in the toolbar.

Shorthands

Type a notation's name wrapped in a border to insert it instantly — for example (frac), (alpha), (sum), (int). The borders ( ), [ ], { } and $ $ all work.

Matrices use a size pattern: (paramatrix3c2r) makes a 3-column, 2-row matrix with parentheses. Swap the prefix for the brackets you want — para = ( ), brck = [ ], vbar = | |.

Open the Shorthand reference tab above to browse and search every shorthand. There you can:

  • Click a symbol to insert it at the cursor.
  • Edit a name to rename its shorthand.
  • Click Borders to add your own border — one character, the same on both sides (e.g. a lets you type afraca).

Copy & paste

Select text and chips (Ctrl+A selects everything), then copy. Chips travel as LaTeX, so pasting into another page rebuilds them, and pasting into a LaTeX or Markdown document renders correctly.

Saving & exporting

Export PDF downloads a PDF of the document.
